01
Executive summary

The answer on one page

The problem

In the chain simulation (20 stores, 129 high-turnover items), 4.1% of sales is lost to stockouts: R$ 2.39 M a year, in line with retail's ~4%. The team finds out after the shelf is empty.

The solution

A model flags which in-stock items will run out in the next 7 days. An AI agent checks the facts and proposes the cheapest fix: transfer, expedite or urgent order. The manager approves in one click. All on a single platform, Databricks.

The value

Up to ⅓ of the loss recovered: R$ 0.4 to 0.8 M a year in the simulation, depending on execution; R$ 0.7 to 1.4 M per R$ 100 M of sales. Measured platform cost: ~US$ 440 a month, at list price.

The ask

A 30-day pilot in 4 stores, with 4 control stores, on real data. The decision to scale comes from LojaBR's own numbers, not from a projection.

06
Where to act first

Idle stock in one store fixes the gap in another

  • 39 transfers suggested in a single day, using other stores' excess.
  • Only within 450 km, never leaving the donor with less than 10 days of stock, and arriving in 1 to 2 days.
  • The map shows stockouts per store and the routes. One click opens the store and its actions.
when a transfer pays off
Each transfer protects R$ 74 on average. It only pays off if the marginal freight costs less than that. So in the pilot we only transfer on routes that already run, such as the DC delivery, or by grouping the items of the same store pair.

Transfers are 18% of the value. Most of it (80%) comes from expediting orders already on their way.

and when it is wrong?
A wrong alert does not become a loss. The donor keeps at least 10 days of stock, and the transferred item sells at the destination store. The worst case is the freight, hence the route rule on slide 7.
how it stays good
The model is retrained every day in the same job, with a new version registered in Unity Catalog. The agent looks up stock, nearby stores and the supplier before recommending, and the system rejects any decision made before that lookup.

success criterion
Cumulative stockout rate over weeks 2–4 ≥ 1.5 p.p. below control, and R$ recovered greater than the cost.
choosing the stores
4 stores with above-average stockouts, across 2 regions and 2 formats. Control is matched on format, region and stockout rate and lives the same month, so seasonality and promotions cancel out.
risks and mitigation
Model is wrong: the manager decides. Adoption: one click, in the morning routine. Freight: existing routes only. Incomplete supplier data: the rule covers it.

Appendix · for the technical team

What sits behind each number

  • Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines: Auto Loader on 7 raw feeds; medallion with 7 data-quality expectations, 0 failures in the last run.
  • Model: gradient boosting on point-in-time features (the open order with its expected arrival, not the actual one). Trained on 153 k cases and tested on 49.7 k from later dates; AUC 0.85; retrained and registered in Unity Catalog on every run.
  • Agent: tool-calling on the Foundation Model API; it queries 3 tools, and the system rejects a decision made before them.
  • Lakebase: serving schema published atomically and app schema with approvals, rejections and the Genie log; reads in ~3 ms.
  • Genie: entity matching, metric definitions and 5 certified SQL queries.
  • App: React + TypeScript / FastAPI; service principal with least privilege; sign-in through the workspace SSO.
  • Evidence as text: executed notebooks with outputs, data-quality results, Genie and app responses, and the cost queries in evidence/.
